## Imprisonment

<DigestibleLink version="1.3.1" title="Imprisonment" />


Earlier this month I was held against my will overnight in the apartment of somebody I did *not* want to be around.

I was in Bangkok for an international tech conference, and visited my old friend who I'll call [Alice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_and_Bob). Alice and I had a pleasant dinner before we were joined by her boyfriend Bob, who I was meeting for the first time. While Alice was Khmer, Bob here was a healthy tall black American.

Together, the three of us decided to go for some drinks and brownies. We had a good time at a local bar with live music, and decided to head to their home to hang out for a bit. It was at their home that the inebriation started to hit hard, and with it I could sense tension in the air. Where was Bob? He had just gone up the elevator with us, but now it was only Alice and me in the apartment. Was he blowing off steam? I asked Alice if everything was all right, and she said yes, they were just worried about me.

Bob finally came in. "This is *exactly* like that time we were in Phuket," Bob says with conviction. He sounded a bit mad. "No it's not, that time was *different*," Alice retorted. "Such and such happened instead." I couldn't tell if they were merely bantering or starting to have a fight. I was well aware that I wasn't sober, and that my lack of sobriety was by far the most likely cause of my discomfort. I wasn't sure what was going on, but I decided it would be better to be unsure at home instead. This wasn't my first rodeo; I'm no stranger to calling it a night, tucking myself safe and sound in bed, and having a laugh about it the next morning.

I called a Grab and told the couple that it was nice seeing them, but I was going to head home.

Bob stopped me. "You *know* you're welcome to stay here, *right?*" Bob asked in a passive aggressive voice. Despite the literal words he was saying, his tone indicated that he was more than done with having me around.

"Yes, of course, I know. I just feel like it would be nice to lay down and, um, uh... just... take a breather at home," I stammer out with a long sigh.

"*Let* me come with you," Bob says, and puts his arm around me as he escorts me out the door. I am very uncomfortable with this physical contact, but it's all right, the Grab is waiting downstairs and I'll be free of him soon.

I get into the Grab -- but before I could close the door, Bob grabs it and *forces his way* into the car. What the fuck? This was clearly a novel situation. I may not have been sober, but I now had hard evidence that it wasn't all just in my head. Something was well and truly wrong.

"We're going together," Bob declares.

"No, I'm going alone to my hotel," I tell him. Go away.

"No." Bob is adamant. "We're *all* going to your hotel."

"Why?" I ask. "What are we going to do there?"

"We're going to *chill*. We're going to *talk*."

I was stunned. "Do you want to exchange contact information? I can give you my business card and we can talk later." I had bought a pack of cool translucent business cards before leaving Cambodia, and later that very week I would be handing them out like candy, as if I were an old-fashioned businessman who stepped into a time machine and got himself lost at a modern tech conference.

"*No.* We're going to *your* hotel and talking there."

Bob certainly didn't seem like he was going to leave the car, so I stepped out, and then he stepped out. Alice had joined us by now.

"There's something I don't understand," I said. They asked what. "Why can't I leave?"

"You *can* leave," Bob insists.

"Then why am I not leaving?" I ask.

"*Because* we want to make sure you're safe."

"But why wouldn't I be safe? This is Bangkok." The only thing making me feel unsafe was Bob himself, but I didn't feel safe enough around Bob to say that to his face.

Alice gave some kind of unconvincing non-answer. We stand around for some time as the two of them talk in hushed tones.

"Let's go back to the apartment," Bob demands all of a sudden.

"What are we going to do there?"

"We're just going to *chill*," Bob says in that voice of his that had turned permanently aggressive.

"What do you want from me?!"

"*Nothing*. I don't want anything from you. We're just going to *talk*."

"What are we going to talk about?" I was so confused as to why Bob was so insistent that he and I *had* to talk this very night. What was it I had done to offend him?

"We're just going to *talk*. We're going to hang out, with your friend *Alice?* You *know* her right? Your '*friend*,' who you came to *Bang*kok to '*hang out*' with?" Okay, okay, I made a stupid joke about the city name earlier in the night, and now Bob is referencing that to tell me what he's upset about. I see where this is going. He's read way too much into the situation and now he's fucking pissed.

"You *know* me, right?" Alice chimes in. "*Right?*" Jesus H. Christ, I don't know what argument you guys just had, but I'm not giving ammo to either side. Whatever the hell is going on between you two, leave me the fuck out of it, okay? No, lady. I don't know you and I don't know him. Leave me alone and let me go home. Except that at this point I'm too terrified to say any of that out loud to two clearly agitated individuals, so I simply shake my head.

"Can you remember anything at all? Do you know your own name?" Fuck, maybe this is it. Maybe if I play dumb, Bob will simply think I had way too much to drink instead of blaming me for doing whatever the hell he thought I did with his girl. I shake my head no.

We stand around for some more time outside. "What do we have to talk about?" I ask Bob again. I was hoping that he would bring it up so that we could just hash things out here.

Bob is evasive. "Let's just go *back* to the apartment. We'll talk there."

"Why can't we talk out here?" I felt much safer talking out here in the open than in a private apartment. I recalled the story of my friend Charlie in Seattle. See, Charlie had been hanging out at his neighbor Dan's late at night when Dan somehow started getting *real* pissed at him after snorting a fat line of coke. Back then, Charlie was the type of person who is fundamentally unwilling to accept the idea that there could be some people who simply didn't like him. Charlie tried to make things right with Dan, but the more he talked, the more pissed Dan got. At some point, Dan snapped and started punching Charlie over and over and over again for hours on end, until dawn broke and Charlie was finally able to stumble back home in time for his girlfriend to catch him before she left for her early morning shift.

She rushed him to the hospital, where they told him his eyes were now permanently oblongated. He needed major jaw surgery, but he could only afford to get the operations that were covered by surgery. The remaining injuries that insurance deemed to be cosmetic in nature, Charlie simply had to live with. As for Dan, his dad was a cop, so he knew the protocol. He skipped town for a few days before turning himself in, whereupon he was officially recorded as passing the drug test with flying colors. The case went to court, but Dan had a good lawyer and probably some connections with the court through his dad. Charlie meanwhile was a perennially broke motherfucker, so unfortunately for him, last I heard he still hasn't seen a single cent from Dan to cover any of his medical costs.

Charlie was one of my best friends in Seattle, and his story sat prominently in my mind. At least out here in the street the outdoor security guards could be an eyewitness to anything that happened, even though they all looked away when I tried to look in their direction with pleading eyes. Not their problem. I mean, fair enough. Why go out of your way to help foreigners who haven't even have the decency to learn the words for "help" or "police" in your language? Fuck, that's right, I didn't even know what the local Thai emergency number was -- and even if I did, I was pretty sure that Bob would snatch my phone out of my hands before I could successfully dial for help. I wanted to be free of this man, but I couldn't figure out a way to escape his clutches myself, and Alice did not seem interested in challenging Bob either. Gosh Alice, what kind of partner have you chosen now?

"What is it that we need to talk about that we can't talk about out here?" I tried asking again.

Bob evaded the question again. "It's nicer inside. There's *AC*, and there's *chairs*. I'm tired. Aren't you guys tired? Let's go *back inside* and sit down."

A white tourist walks by on the road. I look at him and consider dragging a fellow English-speaker into my plight. He strolls ahead completely oblivious. Fuck man, am I really going to ask for a white savior to come rescue me from a black man in an Asian country? No, man, that's just... you know what, just let him go. Bob looks at him as well and stares me down, as if he were daring me to make a move. What am I, too pussy to have a man-to-man conversation with Bob? I gotta drag a stranger into this conversation we're supposed to have?

"Why not just sit down here?" I ask Bob. I sat down on the street. Bob sat down next to me. I continue, "Why are you mad?"

"I'm not mad. *Nobody's* mad," Bob answers in a clearly mad tone.

"Then why do I feel nervous?" I was hoping we'd have a conversation about how his angry tone was making me nervous, but alas Bob was not interested in getting called out. We drop into silence.

I felt dreadfully tired. I closed my eyes and let my head sink forward. It felt good. Maybe I could start meditating until Bob got bored and went away.

Instead, Bob started taunting me. "Let's go *back* to the *apartment!* There's mosquitos here. I see one buzzing right around you right now Amos. Oh, there it is. It's landed on you. It's *biting* you." He paused. Saliva gathered in my mouth, and I swallowed. Every time I swallowed, he would start taunting me again. "Oh there's another one! Another one just landed on you Amos. We could be sitting upstairs, but instead you're getting eaten alive out here." You had to hand it to him, goddamn bastard could tell that I was still conscious and listening to him with every swallow I made.

At some point he finally had enough. "Okay, you ready to head back now? We're going to get up on *three*, alright? *One* --"

Okay, fuuuck. I could tell that if I didn't get up now, Bob was going to forcibly drag me back into their apartment. "*Two* --"

I had better get back on my own two feet so that I could show Bob I still had some fight left in me, however meager it may be. I get myself up and Bob starts walking me back to the apartment. Alice trails behind at a distance.

I get into the elevator with Bob. I turn around to find Alice still standing outside the front door of the building. I look pleadingly at her, as if I could ask her "Why aren't you here with us?" with my eyes alone. All she gives me is a direct wide-eyed stare as the elevator doors close. What the shit? What have they planned? Is this some serial killer shit?

Bob and I walk back to his apartment. I decide that my best chance of survival is to go into full submission mode and make it clear that there's absolutely no point in further beating up someone who's already as pathetic as I am. There was simply no other way around it. No eyewitnesses, not even Alice. I was healthy and well the last that Alice saw of me. Who's to say what happened when Bob and I were alone in his apartment late this night? Whatever Bob wanted to happen to me, I was simply going to have to take it.

As soon as we step into his apartment, Bob asks how I feel.

"Pathetic," I told him, just as I'd planned.

Bob was a good interrogator. "What makes you feel *that*?" And I would have to tell him. He made it clear that he wanted answers. "And why would you think *that*?" He wanted specifics. He wanted to hear me say with my own mouth that I had tried to flirt with Alice, but he also wanted to feign ignorance as if he weren't obviously looking to extract a confession out of me. There was no wiggling out of it. I had to say what he wanted me to say. "I guess, I thought she flirted with me, and you know girls are just flirty by nature, I mean I thought what if I just flirt back, it's harmless right? But I didn't realize she had a partner already -- OH! I guess I should've asked first!" Please, dunce card, save me. I'm just stupid, I'm no threat at all, please.

"No," Bob said softly for the first time since we got back to his home.

"No?"

"No. You did nothing wrong."

"I didn't?" Dunce card *SUCCESS!*

"You did nothing wrong. Everything is all right. Let me give you a hug." Bob leaned in and I hugged him. I was so relieved that my eyes were going to stay spherical after all that I started crying as Bob soothed me and told me about God. Fuuuck, yes, the Bob was finally placated.

Alice finally came home. I relaxed on the couch. "Make yourself comfortable, alright? There's the bathroom if you need it. See you in the morning." Okay, phew, I can be quite comfortable now that I know I'm not getting beaten up.

I go through what I can only describe as "sleep, if you were awake to observe how you sleep." At some point during this wakeful sleep, I get up to go to the bathroom. On my way back to the couch, I pass by their two bedrooms. One bedroom door is closed, the other one -- the one that they'd told me they usually use as their office -- was open. I peer into the open one out of curiosity, and see a white shirt wrapped around an upright human torso. What the fuck? Is that... is that *Bob?* Sitting up looking right at me? I mean, Bob is black and the room is dark, so it *could* be him, but all I can see is a white shirt on a human torso that's facing the exact spot that I'm standing on...

Okayyy, you know what? I'm just going to pretend like I didn't see anything and slink back under the covers on the couch. I'm okay dying in my sleep like my grandpa, and not screaming like his passengers, as that old joke goes.

I briefly fall into actual sleep. Morning rolls around, and I wake up with the sun. I've sobered up quite a bit now -- enough to know that what happened yesterday was definitely *not normal*. What I knew for sure was that Bob had forced his way into my Grab last night, and then forcibly pressured me into spending the entire night at his apartment. Was I still being held against my will in this apartment? I couldn't be sure because I still had no idea what Alice and Bob's motives were.

I remembered that I had a 9 AM meeting with one of the event organizers. Ah, at least there's someone who can report me missing if I don't make it to that meeting. I realized that I didn't even know my address; I only knew it as "Alice and Bob's home." Hell, I didn't even know Bob's full name. The best I could do was to send my WhatsApp location to that organizer and tell her that I was currently being held against my will at this location. She was very confused, and demanded to know if I was still going to be able to make the 9 AM meeting. I reassured her that I absolutely was, but that I needed to call her on the phone to make clear to my captors that I *am* going to head out right now because somebody is going to expect me somewhere real soon. And so we called, and I got up from the couch while talking to her live.

Just as I expected, Bob leapt up as soon as I got up. He was clearly monitoring the situation. Alice waved goodbye, and Bob hugged me one last time before I left. I walked to the elevator half-expecting a last-minute surprise, but was able to get out of the building fine. I had to wait uncharacteristically long for a Grab -- probably because the Grab driver the previous night gave me a 1-star rating (I mean, fair!) and therefore other drivers were more wary of picking such a problem passenger up -- but was finally able to successfully get into a car this time around. *Phew*, I had no idea what the fuck happened but I was most definitely, certainly out of that situation now.

---

If you couldn't tell already, this is what happened from Alice and Bob's perspective:

Alice and Bob are already getting worried about me. I've never been this drunk in front of Alice, and Bob doesn't know me. They walk me out the apartment to the Grab to keep an eye on me, and as they watch me get to the car, Alice becomes convinced that I am in no position to make it home by myself. "What are you doing? *Go*, don't let Amos leave by himself!" Alice chides Bob.

Spurred on by his girlfriend, Bob rushes to catch the car door before I close it. Phew, the situation is under control. Amos is safe. We're all going to stick together. Now all we gotta do is go back home and chill.

Except that Amos has completely lost his mind. He can't remember Alice, he can't even remember his *own* damn name. Oh goddamn, we gotta babysit this fucking guy who doesn't know his own limits. Come on my dude, can't we at least babysit you somewhere more comfortable, with AC and proper chairs and no mosquitos? But okay, if you insist, we'll hang out here in the street instead.

Okay, phew, Amos is finally getting up and going back home. Alice is going to give the bros some privacy as Bob calms Amos back down. Of course, tonight they're simply going to have to sleep with the door open to keep an eye out for their friend Amos, so that he doesn't do anything crazy like bolting out of the apartment in the middle of the night and then disappearing forever. It wouldn't do to have a missing persons case on a good friend. Yeah, it's a lot of work, but *sigh*, what can you do except to avoid having too crazy of a night the next time you're out with Amos? Fucking Amos, man.

---

Both sides started out with an initial interpretation -- for me, it was the paranoia that somebody was mad (and quite unfortunately, due to the inebriation all voices were filtered through to sound extremely sarcastic and passive aggressive to me), and for them, it was the worry that I was not doing okay. From these initial conditions, every next step served only to reinforce our mutual misunderstanding of how the situation was playing out.

For me personally, the forceful opening of the Grab door just as I was on the cusp of ending my night gave me the worst possible confirmation of my fears. I became hyper-aware of every bit of information coming in, and so I latched onto Bob saying that we were going to "talk." I kept wanting to know just what unfinished business Bob had in mind and how he wanted to settle it, but of course he couldn't answer that because for him "talk" was just an offhand example of what we were going to do when we chilled at home. I perceived his inability to answer as evasiveness, and conjured up fantastical explanations of my own. I think something explicit like

> "Look, we understand that you think you feel fine, but that's not our assessment of the situation, and so we can't in good conscience let you go by yourself until you've sobered up sufficiently. Like it or not, you're spending the night with us, and that's just the way it's going to be."

would've helped me understand the situation a lot better. I would've disagreed, but at least the situation would make sense. But of course, I understand that we were all fucked up and communicating poorly -- and besides, the exact phrasing that calms me down might be really triggering for someone else, so there's probably no one-size-fits-all solution for this situation.

It was a harrowing experience at the time, but hilarious in retrospect. I now have a lot more empathy for:

- how women could feel unsafe leaving someone's home
- how suspects could get pressured into a false confession without a hand ever being laid on them
- how scary and helpless it feels to be held against your will by the seemingly arbitrary whims of a private individual

## The Kitchen Mess

<DigestibleLink version="1.3.1" title="The Kitchen Mess" />


A few months ago, I had a neighbor who moved into my apartment complex while she waited for her visa to the US to be processed. Since she was only going to be here in Siem Reap very temporarily, she didn't want to go to the trouble of buying a brand new set of kitchenware and cooking supplies. Naturally, I offered her the use of my kitchen instead.

She cooked some of the best food I'd ever tasted. It was restaurant-quality food in a home setting. I dare say she cooked even better than my own parents. Life was good.

For me, that is. She was majorly stressed over the visa process and the complete lack of feedback from the US embassy. They would not give her even so much as a rough estimate of when she might expect the next step in the process to occur, nor even an approximate figure for how many were in the queue ahead of her or which month's applications they were now processing. I commiserated with her. There was an element of irony in how her misfortunes around the slow US visa process resulted in my great fortune of having regular delicious home-cooked meals.

At dinner one night, we fought. As with most fights involving interpersonal relationships, the content of the fight was relatively trivial, but the fight itself was the expression of deeper underlying tensions that I had been unaware of until we talked again a few days later. As it turns out, one of her biggest gripes with our relationship was that she felt like it was really unfair that she goes the whole nine yards to cook these gourmet meals for the two of us, but I can't even do the basic job of keeping the kitchen clean. Now it was my turn to feel that it was really unfair for her to be blaming me for something that she'd never even brought up. If this was truly such a problem, how was it that she never said a single thing about it until now? She explained that she thought she could just grit her teeth through the situation given that she's about to leave anyways, but it turns out that she couldn't.

Whereas I operate under a model where you generally shouldn't be held accountable for anything you did not explicitly agree to be held accountable for, she operates on a model where you're generally supposed to make reasonable accommodations for the known and stated preferences of others. She would often complain about the kitchen by pointing something out and saying 「[邋遢](https://www.cantonese.sheik.co.uk/scripts/wordsearch.php?level=0)!」 in a seemingly humorous manner, and I would laugh and repeat it after her. I thought this was just sassy banter, and so I would make fun of her zealotry over kitchen stains. It turns out this was her way of telling me that these are the standards to which she wanted the kitchen cleaned, and she was getting really annoyed that I was repeatedly refusing to keep the kitchen clean. She would have to explicitly order me to address specific issues with the kitchen, and found it entirely frustrating that I would never generalize and learn to address these issues on my own. Meanwhile I didn't even realize there was something to generalize; I heard her individual requests and handled them as you might handle someone asking you to pass the salt: a situation-specific problem that someone wants solved, and if they ever want that issue solved again then all they have to do is ask.

I come from an ask culture where it's the responsibility of the person with an issue to make their issue known, or else how is anyone else supposed to know that something was actually bothering them? She comes from a guess culture where what she said was already so very explicit and direct, I mean what more could you possibly need before you finally *got* the fucking message? Somebody putting a megaphone to your ear and blaring out "**I don't like this!**"? Seriously, how many times had she told me that such a dirty kitchen makes her -- somebody who *loves* cooking -- not even want to cook anymore?

And that's just the thing. Even if you make your dissatisfaction with the kitchen abundantly clear to me, my default reaction would be that it's entirely fine and understandable that you don't like this. Mine ain't the best kitchen to cook in, I know. It's small, and it's cramped, and it's got this dried-out mark on the counter and that little bit of chopped onion on the stove from the last stir fry. I mean, I don't mind it personally, but I can see why someone else might. And yeah I get you on not wanting to cook, I don't really feel like it either these days. I'm thinking of ordering in, you want anything?

Oh, what you mean is that you want *me* to make my kitchen into the kitchen that you'd enjoy cooking in? Well why didn't you just say so? I mean, I like delicious food, so even though it's not particularly meaningful for me to keep my kitchen absolutely spotless, I might still find it worthwhile on some days of the week to clean the kitchen to spotless perfection so that I could get a delicious home-cooked meal.

I don't think there's an objective metric for "dirty" or "clean" here. The kitchen exists in some configuration of objective physical state -- she can point to specific details and I'll readily acknowledge that I am observing that specific detail too, but when I observe it my reaction is "Wow she really is a neat freak" and hers is 「邋遢!」. Which definition of "dirty" or "clean" is more correct? I think that depends fundamentally on your personal tastes.

Nor do I think that there's an objective metric for "fair" here. Objectively, a sequence of physical events involving kitchenware, food, and mouths occurred on a regular basis. Subjectively, her perception of a fair bargain was: she cooks good food for the both of us, and in return I restore the kitchen to a state that is conducive to her cooking the next good meal for the both of us. I think that's pretty reasonable: the effort she expends in cooking is way greater than the effort I expend in cleaning, so by this measure even this fair deal is rather lopsided in my favor. My perception of a fair bargain was: I let her do whatever she wants with my kitchen, she cooks an extra portion of food for me, and in return I wash all the dirty dishes. I think that's pretty reasonable too: the time and effort she saves in not having to stock her own kitchen from scratch and not having to do a single bit of cleanup is way greater than the bit of extra time and effort she spends cooking a larger portion of the same meal she was going to cook anyways, so by this measure even this fair deal is rather lopsided in her favor. (We split the monetary costs of the groceries and took turns doing the shopping, depending on our schedule and availability.) Which definition of "fair" is more correct? I think that depends fundamentally on your personal values.

I did not feel myself responsible for her negative internal reaction to how events unfolded. I merely happened to be the conduit through which Universal Misery entered her life, and did not deserve any moral blame in the matter. But even if I did not find myself to be morally in the wrong, here was still a person whose proximate cause of suffering was catalyzed by my behavior. If an apology was what would best ease her suffering, then an apology she shall have. I could not apologize in good faith for wrongdoing because even if I wanted to adopt the guess culture framing, my general sense of obliviousness makes it impractical for me to operate in anything other than ask culture space. What I *could* and did apologize for was my regret in not understanding Eve's intentions earlier -- if I could go back in time knowing what I know now, I would most certainly do things differently. If I had served as a conduit for Universal Misery, I could now serve instead as a conduit for [Universal Compassion](/blog/digestibles/v1.3.0/universal-love-universal-compassion/), and would not deserve any moral praise for that either.

Eve apologized as well, even though I didn't feel like she'd done anything wrong. If there was one thing I was really annoyed by, it was that she had been unjustly mad at me. But now that I understood where she was coming from, I could see that it entirely made sense for her to react in the way she did. And I of course extended to her the same sense of non-responsibility I extended to myself: my own negative internal reaction to the unfolding of events was caused by my own hyper-allergic reaction to anything involving personal blame, and that in turn is due to my personal childhood history with responsibility, not anything to do with her.

There's a [barn and ladder paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladder_paradox) that comes about when you find yourself doing relativistic physics. Suppose there's a dude Frank who's carrying a 3 meter long ladder, and suppose Frank is running towards this girl Grace's tiny 2 meter long barn at nearly the speed of light. Because length contracts for fast-moving objects, Frank is running fast enough that the ladder is now shorter than the barn.

Mischievous Grace has an idea: she's going to press a button that automatically slams both barn doors shut when Frank and his ladder are contained entirely inside the barn, and then a split second later the pre-programmed doors will open again so that Frank can pass right through the barn without injury. The image from Wikipedia explains it best:

<img src="/posts/v1.3.1/contracted-ladder.svg" alt="Ladder contraction" />

Afterwards, Grace giggles and asks Frank if he panicked at all when he found himself completely trapped inside the barn. What does Frank say?

Note that relativity is, well, relative. So from Frank's perspective, he is in a sense standing still, and Grace and her barn are what's moving towards *him* at near light speed. But length contracts for fast-moving objects, and so Grace and her barn are now even *shorter* than they originally were. If Frank's ladder couldn't fit in the barn before, it definitely can't fit in the squished barn now. And yet Reality is objective -- either Frank's ladder collides with one of the barn doors, or it does not. Which is it?

Something has to give. And with relativity, both time and space must give. The only events that have an objective order in relativity are events that are within each other's light cone -- in other words, the events that are capable of affecting each other causally. Nothing can travel faster than the speed of light, and therefore if light couldn't have left event A to get to event B in time, then it is impossible for event A to have had any effect on event B, and the universe is free to reorder these independent events however it likes.

Note that this means "simultaneity" is no longer a workable concept, because light takes a nonzero amount of time to get from one place to another, and therefore two events happening at "the same time" are actually unrelated events that might happen in a different order in a different reference frame.

Here we have four events that objectively happened, that all observers can agree on:

1. The right-side barn door slams shut as the front of the ladder almost reaches it
2. A split second later, the right-side barn door opens again before the front of the ladder smacks into it
3. The left-side barn door slams shut just as the back of the ladder leaves it
4. A split second later, the left-side barn door opens again

Events 1 and 2 are related -- the door must close before it gets opened again, so these events will always appear in this order. Events 3 and 4 are related as well for the same reason. But the first two events are completely unrelated to the last two events, because for this scenario to even be possible, Frank and his ladder have to be traveling so fast that the ends of the ladder have effectively become causally disconnected from each other. (This is actually always the case for all ladders, it's just that the time frame to observe a causal disconnect is impossibly small under normal circumstances.)

And so, from Frank's point of view, he's running through the squished barn when all of a sudden the right-side barn door slams shut right in front of him! Then it quickly opens up again before he crashes into it, and Frank is able to run through without a problem. But just as he's about to be finished with the barn, the left-side barn door slams shut behind his ladder, and opens up again just as quickly.

<img src="/posts/v1.3.1/contracted-barn.svg" alt="Barn contraction" />

"Darn Grace!" Frank mutters. "What's she up to now, opening and closing barn doors at random?"

Grace tells Frank how she'd temporarily trapped him in between the two barn doors. But simultaneity is subjective, and because simultaneity is required for the very concept of being "inside" something, "inside" and "outside" are also subjective. The raw facts of events 1 - 4 are all agreed on, and yet Frank truthfully tells Grace that there was not a single moment where he was fully enclosed by the barn.

My conflict with my friend Eve reminds me of this physics paradox. In one view, you have:

1. the aggrieved parties who interacted,
2. were wronged and hurt by these interactions,
3. and were made whole again through restitution in the form of sincere apologies.

In another view, you have:
