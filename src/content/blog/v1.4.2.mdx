Ever since [*The Ceremony*](/blog/v1.0.0/) happened, I've been publishing a blog post right on the dot every month. Because the v1.0.0 blog post was an expression of my love (for my ex, but also for myself and for life) on Valentine's Day, subsequent blog posts were anchored to the 14th of every month until my birthday last year, after which they were anchored to the 29th of every month instead. Compare that to [how this blog started](/blog/v0.1.0/) one year earlier, back when you could really see the irregular cadence of my habits: I would [start out fine](/blog/v0.1.1/), miss the deadline by [a day](/blog/v0.1.5/) or [two](/blog/v0.1.2/), and then that day would turn into [a week](/blog/v0.2.0/) and that week would end up completely derailing the habit until [the next opportune Schelling fence](/blog/v0.2.1/) of a date.

I ran on deadlines because [as stressful as they were](/blog/digestibles/v1.3.0/the-fruits-of-the-retreat/), deadlines were the only way I ever got anything done. For the last year and a half, I adhered steadfastly to the monthly blog deadline because I knew that if I started slipping, that would be it. That would be the beginning of the inevitable end, at least until another catalyst came along to whip my helpless ass back into shape for a brief facade of executive well-functioning before the cycle repeated itself again.

I did not allow for that to happen. This time with The Ceremony was different. The cycle was not going to repeat, and I was uncompromising. Why I write, I cannot say. All I know is that writing for the world is one of the ways through which my soul breathes, and I must breathe, even if it is through the forcing function of a deadline-based ventilator.

I did not trust myself with the freedom to choose my publishing dates because I had repeatedly demonstrated to myself that [I was not to be trusted](/blog/v1.0.3/) with such freedom. Others could trust me because my promises to them were rock solid, but my word clearly meant nothing to myself. And so this old paradigm of fear and coercion was born, and it worked well in many ways. The results speak for themselves.

It was also highly dysfunctional in many ways. The results speak for themselves.

Perhaps it is time to try something new, and see what happens. I make no predictions about how the future will relate to the past. I simply no longer fear the consequences of letting go. It is time for me to breathe on my own.
