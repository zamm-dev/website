That's only one perspective, of course. The surgery prompted me to reevaluate my relative ordering of values. Health now placed above work. Even though I had already nominally placed work above health before, my executive dysfunction meant that I would procrastinate until I finally panicked because there was no longer enough time in the night to finish everything I needed to do. I would panic and start doing things in a rush, and when push came to shove, my revealed preferences showed that I actually cared more about upholding my professional responsibilities than getting adequate sleep.

I still care about upholding my professional responsibilities. I refuse to be anything but a man of my word. But I also refuse to do anything other than sleep on time. If I fail to get all my work done in time -- and there is only ever a reasonable amount of work to do, the only reason I wouldn't be able to do so is if I procrastinate liberally like I usually do -- well then that's just too fucking bad, health comes first.

In the past I could call my own bluff and I would fold. Not anymore. I can finally stand up to myself now.

My productivity suffered badly for a day. I'd heard about the benefits of coaching; I tried out a life coach to see if she could help. She was a nice person, but her questions were rote and uninspired. It felt as if she were working off of a call center script and could only ever ask me the same questions over and over again no matter what I answered: "What would you like to be different? What are your next steps? What might get in your way?" Maybe those simple questions really are that powerful even when repeated *ad nauseam*, but if that's the case then she should've simply said so and taught me how to apply them effectively to myself. She made me realize that the business version of a ["nice guy"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice_guy) is someone who's really nice as a person but utterly ~unattractive as a date~ incompetent at what they do. No, fuck you, I won't let your niceness guilt-trip me into wasting a single extra cent on your services. I mean, I worded it much more nicely than that, of course, but the point remains. Someone could be a genuinely wonderful human being and still deserve a horrible review for their professional services.

No matter. I've long learned that [I can't outsource fixing myself](/blog/digestibles/v1.1.2/vulnerability/) to therapists, and of course that applies to life coaches as well. The one gold nugget that came out of the hour-long session was that she got me to realize I derived absolutely zero joy from any of the work tasks I did that day. Sending out contracts, writing emails informing people that I have sent out contracts for them to sign, responding to code reviews on PRs where all of the interesting work had already been done -- perhaps someone else would find these sorts of tasks very intellectually stimulating, but for me they came across as uniformly dull and frustrating. How to derive meaning out of meaningless tasks?

Enter Ego. I *need* to succeed now. My business partner and I just landed the biggest contract we've come across so far. All that zen talk about "Everything will be all right in the end, everything's that meant to be will be" or "All which is lacking will be made whole in the afterlife" or "Perhaps in another life, in another time" or "Failure is always an option that will have to be dealt with in one of the Everett branches"? All that is some real *loser* shit, and [I'm a motherfucking winner](/blog/v1.3.2/) dude. This is the only goddamn world that's been empirically shown to exist, this is the only goddamn Everett branch that matters, and this is the only goddamn life I'll ever get to live. This "meaningless" work bullshit *matters*. My Ego *matters*. The stakes are hella real. My future kid is gonna get fucking aborted if I don't pull through, dude.

The only way out is through. **This** is the story of how I finally wrestled my executive dysfunction to the ground with God's unfair assistance to become the motherfucking winner I always knew I was deep down. It all started here.

Watch me win.
